CLASSROOM QUIZ — how to use
===========================

Students answer one question at a time on their phones and see instantly
whether they were right, with an explanation. No internet, no accounts,
no app to install on the phones.


1. FIRST TIME
-------------
- Unzip ClassroomQuiz.zip somewhere you keep files (e.g. Documents). You get
  a ClassroomQuiz folder with the app, questions.xlsx and an images folder.
  Run it from there, not from inside the zip.
- Double-click ClassroomQuiz.exe. A black window opens and your browser
  opens the start page.
- The very first launch can take up to a minute while antivirus checks it.
- "Windows protected your PC"?  Click  More info  ->  Run anyway.
- Windows Firewall asks for access?  Tick  Private networks  and click Allow.
  (Without this, students' phones cannot connect.)
- questions.xlsx already holds sample quizzes showing every question type,
  including picture questions. Try them, then replace them with your own.
  (If questions.xlsx is ever missing, the app makes a fresh starter file.)

Keep the black window open during class. Closing it stops the quiz.


2. YOUR QUESTIONS (Excel)
-------------------------
Each sheet (tab) in the workbook is one quiz; the sheet name is its title.
Row 1 holds the headings, then one question per row:

  Type | Question | Image | A | B | C | D | E | F | Answer | Explanation

- Type: MCQ, True/False, Short answer or Word cloud.
- Image (optional, any type): the file name of a picture, e.g.
  circuit.png. Put the picture in the "images" folder next to the app.
  PNG, JPG, GIF, WEBP or SVG. It appears on the projector, on every phone
  (students can tap it to enlarge) and on your teacher page. A web address
  (https://...) works too, but then the phones need internet.
  Example: part of the periodic table with cells hidden behind A, B, C, D
  and the question "Which label hides a noble gas?" with options A-D, or a
  circuit diagram with "What is the total resistance?" — see the
  "Picture questions" sheet.
- MCQ: put the options in A-F (at least two; leave the rest empty).
  Answer = the letter of the correct option (e.g. B), or its exact text.
- True/False: leave A-F empty. Answer = True or False.
- Short answer: leave A-F empty. Students type their answer.
  Answer = every answer you accept, separated by ;   e.g.  carbon dioxide; CO2
  Capitals, extra spaces and a full stop at the end don't matter,
  and 14 matches 14.0 — but spelling does.
- Word cloud: leave A-F and Answer empty. Students type a word or short
  phrase; the projector shows all answers as a cloud (most common = biggest).
  Not scored. Good for "one word that describes..." questions.
- Explanation: optional, shown to students after you reveal the answer.
- A sheet named "Instructions", or starting with _ , is ignored.

Two ways to use your file:
  a) Edit questions.xlsx next to the app, save, then click
     "Reload questions.xlsx" on the start page (or restart the app).
  b) Click "Load an Excel file..." on the start page and pick any .xlsx.

If a row has a mistake, the start page lists it (sheet name and row number)
and skips just that question. A picture that isn't in the images folder is
listed too; that question is shown without it. "Download template" gives a
blank example. Pictures are read from the images folder whichever way you
load the Excel file.


3. IN CLASS
-----------
1. Start page -> "Start a new session".
2. Click "Open projector display" and put that window on the projector.
   It shows a QR code.
3. Students connect their phones to the SAME WiFi as your laptop and scan it.
4. On your teacher page, pick a quiz. For each question:
   wait for answers -> "Reveal answer" -> "Next question".
5. At the end students see their score; the projector shows the class chart.

TYPED ANSWERS (Short answer / Word cloud): the teacher page shows
"Answers so far" — click it to see what students typed, before revealing.
It starts closed, so nothing shows if your laptop screen is mirrored.
  - Hide: keeps an answer off the projector (e.g. a silly word).
  - Accept (Short answer only, before you reveal): counts a near-miss such
    as a spelling slip as correct for everyone who typed it.

LEAGUE (TOP 10): every student gets a random 3-digit number (e.g. #427) shown
on their phone — no names are collected. After each reveal and at the end,
the projector and phones show the top 10: most correct answers first, ties
won by whoever answered correctly faster. Arrows show who moved up or down,
and each student also sees their own rank (e.g. "Rank 14 of 40").
Word clouds don't count, so a quiz of 10 questions with 2 word clouds is
scored out of 8.


4. PHONES CAN'T CONNECT?
------------------------
- Check "Students connect via" on the teacher page. If your laptop has
  several networks, pick the WiFi (or hotspot) the phones are on.
- Some college WiFi blocks phones from reaching laptops. Turn on your
  laptop's Mobile Hotspot (Settings -> Network -> Mobile hotspot), have
  students join it, and pick the hotspot address (often 192.168.137.1)
  under "Students connect via".
- Make sure the firewall prompt was allowed for Private networks, and that
  your WiFi is set to Private, not Public.


Only the laptop running the app can load questions or control the quiz;
students on the WiFi can only join and answer.
